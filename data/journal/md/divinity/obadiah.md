# Obadiah – The Silent Watcher

*Minor Prophet of oversight, silent judgment, and post-conflict truth*

## Domain & Aspect

Obadiah is the Minor Prophet of oversight and aftermath. He bears silent witness to divine judgment, conflict, and ruin. His domain is not vengeance, but the undeniable truth that follows it — memory, consequence, and echo.

## Manifestation & Rebirth

Obadiah prefers scavenger forms, most commonly a towering roc-like vulture with ash-gray wings and a polished black skull. When reborn he may return as a carrion lion, a bone serpent, or another beast of the aftermath. He rarely interacts directly, often seen circling far above sites of devastation, prophetic collapse, or corrupted prophecy. When slain, he dissolves into smoke and feather, reborn at sites of unburied truth or near Lake Genesis — but only once divine silence has passed.

## Behavior & Influence

He does not intervene in conflict — he arrives after. He will not speak; his presence compels memory and restores lost names or severed truths. Mortals report feeling "re-aligned" or "seen" after his gaze falls upon them.

Obadiah does not retaliate in violence — his gaze alone reshapes narrative. He reveals truths others tried to bury, undoes false histories, and calls the dead to bear witness. Some false priests have been unmade simply by his passing shadow.

## Followers & Taboos

Obadiah has no temples, only shrines made from battlefield remains. His followers include the **Truthwalkers**, pilgrims who carry fragments of prophecy to sacred ruins; the **Bonebinders**, morticians and truth-tellers who refuse to bury lies; and the **Echo-Scribes**, archivists who only write what his shadow has passed over. He serves Ezekiel as a silent vector of after-clarity.

Speaking over the dead, distorting truth in eulogy or aftermath, or touching bones before a shadow has passed are taboo. Breaking these brings the spiral ash — and a memory you thought was buried.

## Lore & Myth

Some say Obadiah once circled the fall of a Prophet and carried that Prophet's name back to the world. Others claim he is the only beast False Gods cannot lie before, though none know why. Some sects believe Obadiah was once human, a mourner who followed Ezekiel too long; others believe he is Ezekiel's "other eye," cast out to witness what the Prophet cannot. His silence is not void — it is weight.
