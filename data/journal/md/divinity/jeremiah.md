# Jeremiah – The Silent Tortoise

*Major Prophet of sorrow, divine grief, and slow judgment*

## Domain & Aspect

Jeremiah is the Major Prophet of sorrow, divine grief, and patient judgment. Known as the Silent Tortoise, he carries the weight of Behel'ith's unhealed wounds upon his back, and remembers every failure so none are forgotten. His presence is mournful and immense — a still tide of consequence that cannot be rushed or evaded.

## Manifestation & Rebirth

Jeremiah takes the form of a titanic tortoise with an ancient moss-grown shell and mournful eyes of stormlight. When he moves, the earth listens. He is rarely seen in motion, most often glimpsed resting at the edge of sorrow — near battlefield craters, fallen temples, or mass graves.

When destroyed, his shell breaks into seven divine shards, carried by birds to sacred mountains, where he reforms only after the winds of grief pass through them.

## Behavior & Influence

Jeremiah moves slowly but never halts, delivering judgment long after others have fled or forgotten — often when the pain is deepest. He weeps quietly where others rage; his tears salt the earth, purify lies, and remind the faithful.

He does not destroy — he mourns. He calls the winds to wail through ruins, sends divine birds to carry messages of sorrow or final truths, and causes weapons to rust in the hands of murderers, and oaths to crumble into silence. Those who invite his judgment are not struck — they are left behind by hope.

## Followers & Taboos

His sacred flock, **The Mourning Flight**, are avian-beast messengers and divine watchers who deliver his laments across Behel'ith, often singing or crying aloud in strange tongues, recording grief in the wind and nesting only where memory still weeps. His minor prophets — Habakkuk, Nahum, and Zephaniah among them — are instruments of mourning and memory, not war.

Laughing at the innocent's pain, discarding the names of the dead, or interrupting sacred silence are grave offenses. Those who offend Jeremiah find themselves unable to speak, dream, or feel warmth until their soul is washed in honest grief.

## Lore & Myth

It is said Jeremiah once refused to move for forty days, causing a False God to starve for attention. Some believe he carries a library carved inside his shell, where the names of every unjust death are kept in silence — he is often linked to the Final Mercy, a divine act where sorrow itself purges sin. Some believe Jeremiah is the first sorrow of Behel'ith, a divine regret given form; others claim he will be the last Prophet standing at the island's end, watching the stars grieve their children.
