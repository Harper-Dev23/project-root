# Nahum – The Divine Enforcer

*Minor Prophet of divine retribution, swift justice, and heavenly wrath*

## Domain & Aspect

Nahum is the Minor Prophet of swift divine justice and final consequence. Known as the Divine Enforcer, he embodies judgment in its purest form — decisive, blazing, and unstoppable. Where prophecy has been ignored or corrupted, Nahum falls like fire.

## Manifestation & Rebirth

He takes the form of a radiant griffon — lion and eagle, glowing with celestial sigils and wrathful brilliance — appearing amid divine storms, blazing skies, or when the Psalms thunder in the distance. He is reborn from scorched skies or bones left unjudged, reappearing only once true injustice demands it, and shifts form only in death, when his purpose is spent.

## Behavior & Influence

Nahum appears only when judgment is ready to be enforced. He does not speak — his roar silences the whispers of False Gods. He is seen as a living verdict; once he arrives, there is no appeal.

He strikes with righteous violence: wings of flame descend with winds that break oaths, claws burn symbols into stone where corruption stood, and cities may see a single streak of fire across the sky and know what has been ended. None are warned twice.

## Followers & Taboos

He is revered by militant sects and judgment cults within Jeremiah's broader flock, represented by burning feathers, scorched statues, and obsidian talons embedded in altars. Legends say only Jeremiah himself can recall Nahum once summoned, and even he hesitates.

Denying judgment once accepted, betraying divine law after forgiveness, or interrupting Nahum's descent with false pleas are taboo. Those who attempt to stop him risk more than death — they may be erased from divine memory entirely.

## Lore & Myth

Nahum is said to have cleaved the sky open once to descend upon an army of heretics — both sword and storm, and where he passes, nothing impure remains. Some believe his form was gifted by Jeremiah, created not in grief but in holy rage. Some claim Nahum was once a False God, reformed by grief into purpose; others say he will one day judge the other Prophets, should they stray. He is the roar at the end of the storm.
