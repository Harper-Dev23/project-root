# State of the World

Where things stand for a Hunter arriving on Behel'ith now.

## The island is sealed

Behel'ith is cut off from the rest of the world by divine will, and it is not inert. Mountains hum with scripture, rivers forget their paths, and the land keeps a record of everyone who walks it -- particularly those who try to change it. Some ground blesses. Some curses.

## The covenant still holds

Four tribes once stood on the edge of war. Rather than fight it, their elders sent champions across the sea to settle the matter through the Hunt instead. That arrangement has not broken. [[Camp Nehemiah]] exists because of it -- the one place on the island where rival tribes share a fire without drawing blood.

The tribes have not stopped competing; they have only agreed on where. See [[The Four Tribes]].

## The divine is not settled

Above the tribes sit the **Prophets** -- divine beings wearing the shape of great beasts, ancient and aware. Against them stand the **False Gods**, and between the two the island tilts back and forth. Neither side is finished.

Most of what a Hunter learns about either comes secondhand, from [[The Seers]], from [[Samuel Mourne]], or from things met in the field that fit neither category: [[Augustine]] in the eastern tributaries, [[Esther]] at the boiling edge of the [[Springs of Sheol]], [[Lamentation]] coiled in the dark.

## What is open, and what is not

Very little of the island is reachable yet. Two grounds are sanctioned for new Hunters -- [[The Reeds of Gethsemane]] and the [[Bay of Solace]] -- and the rest is charted but closed. The Hunt has to reach them first.

The [[The Waystone]] tracks that opening. Every Hunter carries a shard of it, and every shard is watching the same slow network come awake.

## What a Hunter does about it

Not much, at first. You train in the [[The Combat Pit]], you take work from the tribes, you go out through the [[The Hunt Gate]] and you come back with what you can carry. Standing accumulates. Ground opens.

Nobody at camp pretends this is the whole story. They only decline to explain the rest of it until you have survived enough of it to be worth telling.
