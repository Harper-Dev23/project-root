# The Animated Six

_Six animated constructs, real combat AI._

The first genuine fight in the pit, and a deliberate jump. Six constructs animated into distinct class roles, each running the same decision-making a real combatant would: they pick targets by threat and opportunity, hold abilities for the right moment, and — the part that catches people — **they respond to each other.**

## How the fight works

Treat this as six interlocking problems rather than six health bars.

- **[[Chad the Unbreakable]]** is the wall, and he does not merely soak damage — he can **redirect a blow onto himself** before your roll resolves. An attack aimed carefully at a fragile target in the back can simply land on Chad instead, at his defences rather than theirs.
- **[[Stan, of the Light]]** channels real healing across the party and cleanses weakness meters off his allies. He prioritises the lowest-health wounded ally and will not waste a cast on someone already at full, so chip damage spread across the group is close to worthless — he will simply top it back up.
- **[[Gary the Grim]]** and **[[Lenny the Magnificent]]** are the casters, and they fight with layered tactics rather than repeating one spell. Lenny channels a large area effect that must be answered; Gary works in curses and necrotic damage.
- **[[Doug Longshot]]** and **[[Shifty-Eyed Mo]]** are reactive. Both answer the moment an ally is struck. Mo's answer is the dangerous one: a curse that lands **before your attack rolls**, weakening the strike that triggered it rather than the next one.

The through-line is that damage alone does not close this fight. Stan undoes spread damage, Chad absorbs precision, and Mo taxes aggression. You have to break the support structure or commit hard enough to outpace it.

## What it teaches

Focus fire, and the order you break a formation in. This is the encounter where "kill the healer" stops being a cliché and becomes arithmetic — and where you learn that the tank's job is to make that plan not work.

## Damage typing

Weighted toward physical, with genuine necrotic pressure from Gary's curse kit. If you are testing resistances, this is a reasonable place to feel the difference between Physical and Necrotic mitigation.

## Reckoning Ladder

Each tier is unlocked by clearing the BASE encounter, not the tier below it — the ladder is not a chain, so a tier can be attempted in any order.

This ladder scales the same roster rather than replacing it. Health is a multiplier on each combatant; resists and Resilience are added on top of whatever they already had.

| Tier | Health | Added resists (P / E / N) | Added Resilience | New at this tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reckoning I | ×1.5 | +8 / +8 / +8 | +20 | - |
| Reckoning II | ×2.2 | +20 / +20 / +20 | +45 | - |
| Reckoning III | ×3 | +35 / +35 / +35 | +70 | - |

## Rewards

XP is awarded every time the fight is cleared, not only the first. Reckoning Marks are the same — they are the currency the pit pays out for repeating its harder work, and they are spent at the bone pile.
