# Levelling and the Awakening

## Levelling

A Hunter who gains a level receives **stat points** to spend across the six core attributes, allocated from the level-up screen. Nothing is committed until it is applied, so a spread can be rearranged freely up to that point.

Because every derived value comes from those six attributes, a point is never only one thing. A point of CHA is Initiative *and* Max MP *and* two resists at once; a point of WIS is Elemental Resist, Resilience and stronger healing together. Spending is better read off the [[Stat Calculations Overview]] than guessed at.

## The Awakening

Beyond the base classes lies the **Awakening** -- a ritual in which a Hunter transcends their starting class and commits to one of three specialised paths.

Each base class has three Awakenings, eighteen in all. Each is laid out as a constellation of **twelve nodes**: three entry, four adept, four master, and a single capstone.

The constraint that shapes all of them is that a Hunter earns only **eight points** -- one per level from level 3 through level 10 -- against those twelve nodes. No tree can be filled. Every one of them forks, so the question is which route to commit to, not how far along a single line you get before running out.

A few nodes hang off a tree without being a prerequisite for anything beyond them: visible connections that reward touching two branches instead of driving deeper into one.

The three paths open to each base class are listed under **Awakenings**:
[[Awakenings: Shepherd]], [[Awakenings: Scholar]], [[Awakenings: Grunt]], [[Awakenings: Beggar]], [[Awakenings: Acolyte]] and [[Awakenings: Performer]].

### Where it stands

The Awakening is **not yet implemented**. The paths, their names and their shapes are settled, and all three for a given class can be previewed from the Talents tab of the level-up screen -- but no Awakening can be undergone and no node can be spent. Nothing on those trees affects a Hunter in the field yet.
