# The Judges

On the southeastern rim of Lake Genesis, visible from the Writhing Hills, three towering stone spires rise from a crescent ridge of pale rock — carved by wind and time into harsh, upright verticals. The air around them is unnervingly still, as if waiting on a verdict that hasn't yet been spoken. The Spires stand apart yet aligned, shaped almost like cloaked figures, some leaning slightly as though listening; narrow slits called the Chime-Clefts emit harmonic tones when the wind passes through them, turning discordant whenever falsehood draws near. A raised platform known as the Shard Dias is said to make truth fracture visibly when spoken aloud upon it.

No living residents are known to remain here — only, as locals put it, the presence. Pilgrims occasionally climb the ridge seeking absolution; some descend changed, and some do not descend at all. At twilight, a few report a woman's voice murmuring beneath the wind near the center spire — what she says is never remembered, only that it felt like permission. Phantom echoes of long-past trials are said to drift through the spires at dawn, repeating names no one has ever heard before.

The Judges claim no affiliation with any prophet or false god, yet their silence feels watched. Some say they are the last three angels who refused to choose a side in the Divine War, rooted in stone as eternal witnesses; others believe they were once human prophets bold enough to judge gods. The most feared version of the story, rarely spoken aloud, holds that the whispers belong to a false god never named — and that the Judges serve her not through worship, but through omission.
