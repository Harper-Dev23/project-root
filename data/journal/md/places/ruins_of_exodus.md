# The Ruins of Exodus

Directly east of Lake Genesis, straddling the River Wormwood, the Ruins of Exodus are a vast, partly sunken ruin of stone and moss-covered marble spread across the river's banks. By day it feels almost peaceful; after dark, the ruins stir with unspoken memory. At the heart of the site stand the Stones of Levithica — ten monumental pillars arranged in a perfect circle, each marked with faint runes that glow softly indigo when touched, warm beneath a pious hand and cold beneath a false one. Broken causeways, once bridges and stairs across the river, now hover in places inches above the water with no visible support, and a flooded sanctum — a half-collapsed basilica of unknown age — lies submerged at the eastern edge, its stained-glass windows depicting faces no one recognizes.

By daylight the ruins host wandering scholars and silent beasts that rest near the stones without fear. After nightfall, Veil-Stalkers — blank-faced spirits drawn to anyone who speaks a name aloud — move through the ruins alongside Echo Beasts, aggressive incorporeal remnants of the city's final hours, and the Shroudborne, prophets who failed some ancient ritual and now haunt the circle they once tried to complete.

The ruins are believed to carry an echo of divine sorrow that traces back to no known prophet. The Stones of Levithica resist translation, though some scholars argue their arrangement forms a celestial cipher. Fragments suggest the ritual tied to the stones required a name, a blood offering, and the silence of a full moon — and that anyone who speaks falsehood inside the circle may find their voice does not return. It's said Exodus was humanity's first city, founded before memory and abandoned when the stars wept; others claim the River Wormwood carved these ruins out of grief, after the first lie was ever told.
