# Islet of Job

In the island's southeast, the Islet of Job is a small, weathered scrap of land surrounded by shifting shallows and a near-constant mist. A sandbar connects it to the mainland, but only at low tide — access is treacherous and timebound, and some say the sandbar vanishes entirely whenever false gods draw near. The air is quiet but heavy, charged with the sense that something unseen is always watching. Whispered prayers, called the Echo Psalms, ride the wind from no discernible source, and bleached standing stones are arranged in a spiral on the northern bluff, their origin and markings unknown.

No prophet is known to claim the islet, yet it is steeped in a kind of silent divinity — a place of spiritual reckoning where visions are rare but profoundly life-altering. The veil between mortal thought and divine indifference is said to be thinnest here, thin enough to hear, though not always to be heard back. Those who receive visions on the islet often return changed — or do not return at all. Local sayings hold that when the gods fell silent, they whispered to the waves, and the waves taught the wind; that one should not cross the bar when faith falters; and that some return with light in their eyes, some with hollowness, and some not at all.
