# Bay of Solace

Curving along the southeastern coast between the Islet of Job and the Whispering Shoals, the Bay of Solace is glass-calm tidal shallows and wind-worn dunes — quiet enough that danger here always feels like a surprise. The water is so clear that depth can only be judged by cloud shadows, and the open, flat coastline makes for easy travel, though the lack of cover means fewer places for trouble to hide and fewer chances for it to find you.

Shells strung by unseen hands form tide-chimes that sound out gentle lullabies along the shore, and cairn-stones along the dunes are engraved with the final words of pilgrims who came here to die in peace. At night, bioluminescent haven fish gather into schools that drift like slow constellations across the bay. Aging pilgrims seeking a peaceful end are drawn here, gentle beasts approach without fear, and children are sometimes guided to its shores in states of prophetic sleepwalking.

The bay is held to be neutral ground, shaped by a collective spirit of solace rather than any one prophet's hand — wounds are said to close faster here, and grief softens, though it never fully disappears. It is said that death itself is restful in the Bay of Solace, and that souls who pass here go straight into dreams without crossing darker waters.
