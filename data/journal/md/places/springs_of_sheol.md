# Springs of Sheol

Amid the blinding cold and howling winds of the Frozen Steppes' northern plateaus, a cluster of steaming sulfuric springs carves out an unnatural warmth. The ground hisses, the air stinks of scorched blood and minerals, and the pools run deeper than they have any right to. No birds nest here, and no footprints last in the ash-crusted snow around them. The Maw Pools are seven hot springs, each a different hue, boiling up from some fire deep underground; jagged basalt channels called the Veins of Char glow faintly and pulse with what feels like a rhythmic hunger, and the Votive Path — ancient stones arranged in a half-circle, cracked by centuries of heat — was once walked blindfolded by pilgrims bearing offerings.

The springs are believed to veil the slumbering presence of Molech, the false god of consumption, his fire-blooded body coiled beneath the rock, hungering for destiny rather than mere flesh. The waters are said to boil from his breath rather than any ordinary geothermal vent; the warmth draws in lost souls, who vanish into the steam, and those who bathe without the proper ritual are said to return changed — or not at all. Signs of Molech's presence are said to include whispered cries in the bubbling steam, skin that chars but does not burn, and visions of paths never taken. As the local sayings go: he does not burn you, he burns your then; when Molech stirs, mountains shed their age; and the Springs do not cleanse — they cauterize.
