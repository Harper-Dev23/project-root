# Verdant Shroud

Southeast of the Forest of Eden, the Verdant Shroud is an overgrown rainforest that seems shaped by deliberate geometry rather than chance — plants bloom in spirals and grids, and at dusk their spores glow faintly, tracing lines that some claim are invisible leylines made briefly visible. Bark on certain trees forms evolving puzzle-glyphs said to gate the deeper paths, and the roots of the Wylett burial groves are believed to hold ancestral songs in a kind of hidden resonance.

The jungle is not empty: plant-spirits seem to debate among themselves in the rustling leaves, illusion-mimics drift through wearing the shapes of old memories, and stewards associated with the prophet Daniel are said to move through the canopy cataloguing the jungle's strange, recurring equations. The prevailing belief is that Daniel's influence favors logic over force here — every step is measured, and intent matters more than strength.

There is an old saying among those who know the Shroud: the jungle is said to "think," and some believe that, given enough time, it means to rewrite the world into a more perfect order.
